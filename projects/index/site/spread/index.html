The desk

For the technical juryEngine room. Peer reviewers may admire the maths from a safe distance.

One formula makes every quote, and a car likely to be late costs more

price = argmaxm  P(sale | m, today's shift) × (m × list price − C)

C = typical fee × (1.2 + 0.8 × p × won cars without a driver) + €150 × p

p the chance the order ends late: a logistic fit over 94,000 won orders, from what is known at the quote

P(sale) the win curve, fitted on 50 weeks of random prices and shifted each day by that day's wins and losses

€150 the refund and the dealer's lost trust

0.4 ms a quote: no model on the quote path

The day's market is read from the day's answers. At one fixed price the desk won 3% of its quotes on the worst tenth of days and 60% on the best; shifting the win curve by each day's answers gave €5,609 against €4,627 a week on 10 held-out weeks. The first desk made €12,376 a week on 120 unseen practice weeks, +⁠€1,486 (1,297 to 1,677) over the desk before it, ahead in 117 of 120.

The number of drivers

For the technical juryFine print. Peer reviewers: fewer drivers, stricter rules. That's the slide.

The week's rules are chosen from the number of drivers, before the first quote

What the desk does

It reads the driver list first. Under 760 drivers it counts every won car still without a driver, and caps any day at 62 won orders to deliver.

What we saw

Merge 1, which counted only cars due within a day, failed 3 of 60 thin weeks: with few drivers it won more cars than they could move, piled up on one due day.

With and without it

0 of 60

unseen thin weeks under 90% on time, the lowest at 92.0%, at a cost of €1,290 a week there. The day cap took one thin week from 88.6% to 95.2% on time: 36 → 16 late orders, and 13 more orders.

The newest, and least certain

For the technical juryFine print. Peer reviewers: we're not sure either, and we say so.

An on-time budget: the final's newest part, and the one we're least sure of

What the desk does

Under 720 drivers and from 820 up, it projects the week's final on-time share at each quote, from the orders already late plus the open orders at their fitted late rates. While that is safely over 90%, it lowers the late cost in the quote, to as little as 0.5 ×.

With and without it

+€307

a week (+11 to +603) on its first 39 paired weeks, but +⁠€159 (−48 to +363) on all 74. The range crosses zero, so the gain isn't proven.

What would break it

89.2%

on time on one practice world with 665 drivers, a week merge 8.2, the same code without the budget, held at 90.55%.

What didn't work

What didn't work, what would break the desk, and how it survives a restart

Lost in play

Cheaper quotes on their own. Lower opening fees on their own. Bigger pushes for volume. Every fee rule for days when drivers drop their gigs.

Would break it

A night when drivers drop their gigs. Such a wave hits cars already won, so no price can reach it.

Survives a restart

26.1 s

In the Sunday 01:00 Helsinki test final we killed the desk on purpose. The standby joined 26.1 s later with 5 requests unquoted, and the week was ranked at 96.4% on time, 20th of 41.
