# Kutsu

**What it is.** The City of Espoo's event organisers start every recruitment event from scratch,
and each side waits for the other: companies ask which candidates will be there, candidates wait for
the company list. Kutsu starts the next event from the last ones. It suggests which event to hold,
which employers to invite and which people, and gives each employer a sign-up that is already filled
in from what it has said in public. The software does the finding, filling in and counting; people
send the invitation, do the meeting and make every judgement about a person.

**Who uses it.** First the organiser, with the people who market the event: at a desk, choosing the
next event and working through its employers, a few minutes each. Second the company contact: opens
one link, once, probably on a phone, and wants to say "yes" or fix a line in under a minute.

**What the officer must be able to do in seconds.** See which company this is. Read its needs. See
the sentence behind any claim. Change a word. Send it. See what came back.

**Product truth that design must not blur.**
- Needs are inferred. Statements are quoted. The two are never presented as the same kind of thing.
- Every quoted statement was found word for word on the page it links to; statements that failed
  that check, or that named a person, were dropped.
- A brief is a draft until the company confirms it, and the screen always says which it is.
- Some companies have almost no public record. The screen says so instead of padding.

**Scope.** A feature demo: Espoo's events, one event's two lists, and the company's sign-up. No
accounts and no settings. People appear only as counts from a sample pool; nothing ranks a person.

**Voice.** Plain, short, specific. Controls name their action ("Send to Vexlum").
