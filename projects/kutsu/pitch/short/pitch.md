# Kutsu, the short pitch

A seven-slide version of the Kutsu pitch, about two and a half minutes spoken. The deck is
[`index.html`](index.html) in this folder; it uses the main deck's stylesheet and screenshots
(`../img/`). The full twelve-slide deck stays at [`../index.html`](../index.html).

The thread through it: with AI every CV reads well, so a CV is only a baseline and the real signal
shows when people meet. That makes meeting in person worth more. Focused in-person events are the
best format but the hardest to set up. Kutsu does the setup, so the time goes to the people.

## What to say

**1. Recruitment is hard. Kutsu makes it easier.**
Recruitment is hard, and it is getting harder. Kutsu takes the setup work off the City of Espoo's
recruitment events, so the city can hold many focused, well-matched ones and spend its time on the
people.

**2. A CV is only a baseline. The real signal shows in person.**
AI helps everyone polish a CV, so every CV reads well and they start to look alike: a lot of noise
and very little signal. A CV was never a good measure of skill anyway; it is a baseline, a reason to
talk. Espoo's organisers say a good match takes skill, motivation and fit, and all three show in a
few minutes face to face. So meeting in person is worth more than ever.

**3. Matching is hard, because it takes people connecting.**
A CV only gets someone to the starting line. A real match needs the right people in the same room,
and every meeting costs time, so who meets whom has to be right. Today it is arranged by hand.
Companies have to be chased down, and say what they need only just before the event. Job seekers
can't tell which companies match them. Organisers pair people by hand, often without the tech
background to judge an application. And every event starts from zero: the sign-up survey and both
invitation lists are built again.

**4. Big events are bloated. Focused events are laborious.**
At a big event, employers are swamped by irrelevant approaches and spend a day at a stand to find a
few people. A focused event, on one theme, fixes that, since almost every conversation is relevant.
But it takes the same setup as a big one, so the city can afford only a few. The format that works
best is the one that is hardest to run.

**5. Kutsu: less time on setup, more time with people.**
This is the core of it. AI drafts, people confirm. A company's sign-up arrives filled in from its
public pages, and a job seeker's from sources they choose; each side confirms every line and says
what it wants, so joining takes a minute. Kutsu watches companies' public pages, so the data keeps
itself current and the next event suggests itself. And the match on paper is written out in words,
with no scores, so the meeting is where skill, motivation and fit come through.

**6. Two pools break the chicken-and-egg problem.**
Companies want to know who is coming before they commit, and people want to know which companies
come. Kutsu keeps a company pool, 1,081 Espoo companies checked from public sources, 46 of them
hiring for 208 tech roles right now, and a talent pool of past attendees who agreed to be invited
again. So before anyone signs up, a company hears "173 people in our pool work in AI and data", and
a job seeker hears that where a role names a language, four in five say English is enough. Each
focused event draws on both pools and adds to them.

**7. More focused events, more real conversations.**
Connections get easier to make, and the time goes to them. Kutsu runs today on real public company
data and synthetic people, with no scores and no automated decisions. Next: a pilot on a short
series of focused events, with Espoo's own past-attendee list and consent texts. And then, after a
beat: "Connecting people".

The closing line nods to the slogan of Nokia, which has its head office in Espoo. Say it lightly,
without naming the company; the slide shows no logo or artwork.

## Where the numbers come from

- 1,081 companies checked, 46 hiring for 208 tech roles, 97 of the 121 roles that name a language
  saying English is enough (80%): Kutsu's company research run, `projects/kutsu/README.md`,
  "Headline numbers".
- 1,140 past attendees and 173 in AI and data: the sample talent pool, `projects/kutsu/pool.json`.
  Nobody in it is real, and the slide says so.
- The quotes on slides 3 and 4: the City of Espoo's challenge brief, "Rethinking Recruitment".
- Skill, motivation and fit as what makes a match, "every event starts from zero" and the
  chicken-and-egg problem: what Espoo's organisers told us,
  `projects/kutsu/NEXT.md`.
