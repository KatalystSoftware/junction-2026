Problems

Problems

Good events, weak matches, and every event starts from zero. The quotes are from Espoo’s challenge brief.

1

Not enough matches come out of the events

Challenges remain in facilitating the right kinds of matches between recruiting companies and jobseeking professionals.
2

Recruiters do not spend time writing what they want

Companies do not always know how to describe their talent or service needs and provide this information too late, only just before the event day.
3

Past contacts are lost between events

Every invitation list is built again from nothing, so companies stay difficult to engage and motivate to participate.

4

Reviewers lack the expertise to judge and matchmake

Organisers have limited ability to understand applicants' real capabilities… especially in technology sectors.
5

AI-written applications meet AI screening

CVs, applications and messages are increasingly generated with AI, while employers are increasingly analysing them with AI, creating a risk that matching decisions are based on synthetic representations rather than genuine capabilities and needs.
Solution

Solution: Kutsu

Solution

Step 1:
Choose which events to hold

Decide the next event on data, not on a hunch.

  • You see suggested event ideas, drawn from past events and public signals (funding, growth, new roles), each with its employer and attendee counts.
  • You see which employers are behind each idea and the news line that put them there, with a footnote to the source.
  • You pick the theme and the date.

Kutsu: suggested event ideas, from the live prototype.

Solution

Step 2:
Invite the right companies and people

You get a suggested invite list and press send yourself.

  • You see employers suggested from what is public about them, and send each a one-click invitation with its sign-up prefilled.
  • You reach consented past attendees by field, shown as counts only. No individual is scored or profiled.
  • You choose which past events and fields to reach, write the message, and see the number it goes to before sending.

Kutsu: employers coming and people to invite for one event.

Solution

Step 3:
Research any public company

Type a company name and get its page drafted from public sources, with a footnote on every fact.

  • You can search any company; a new one is researched for you on demand from the company register, its website, careers page and news.
  • You get a footnote on each field. A claim that cannot be quoted word for word from its source is dropped, not guessed.
  • You send the company its draft; it confirms or corrects it.

Kutsu: a company not in the list yet is researched on demand (left); a drafted company page, every fact with its footnote (right).

Solution

Step 4:
Send prefilled quiz pages

You send each side a few minutes of questions, prefilled from what already exists.

  • You send recruiters their needs drafted from their own job ads; they tick what they hire for at this event.
  • You send attendees the questions this event's companies asked, prefilled from sources they chose to share.
  • They keep or take out every line. You never ask for a photo, age, gender or nationality.

Kutsu, the invitee’s page: nothing is read until they say so; then every drafted line, ticked, to keep or take out.

Solution

Step 5:
Decide who gets in, with AI support

You decide every application; the AI only drafts.

  • You see the AI summary labelled as a draft, kept apart from the lines the applicant confirmed.
  • You get the fit for each employer spelled out in words: what overlaps, what is missing, what to check. No score, no percentage, no ranking.
  • You accept or decline under your own name, with a note for the record. You give employers their own view: only the people who asked to meet them.

Kutsu: the applications queue, and fit written as overlap, gap and check.

Solution

Step 6:
Collect feedback after the event

The day after the event, you ask both sides how the conversations went.

  • You send two questions and a free text box, automatically, to every attendee and company.
  • You read the free text and see the ticks counted: which themes and which employers led to relevant conversations.
  • Those counts feed your suggestions for the next event.

Responsible by design

Responsible by design

Built so a city can actually run it.

No automated decisions

People are never scored, ranked or labelled. A named organiser makes each decision and writes down why.

Read only with consent

An attendee chooses which of their own sources are read, once, and confirms every line before anyone else sees it.

As little data as possible

No photo, age, gender or nationality. A CV, if offered, is read in the browser and not sent. Groups smaller than five are never shown as a count.

Names hidden until decided

Reviewers see what a person can do before who they are, and decide on that.

Fairness check on groups

Per company, who was introduced is compared with who asked, for career changers and people without Finnish. A gap prompts a second look, and companies are asked whether Finnish is truly required.

Built for the AI Act’s high-risk duties

Recruitment AI is high-risk under the Act. The prototype has human oversight, a decision log and labelled AI drafts; retention rules and the conformity assessment are pilot work. All personal data in it is synthetic; company data is public.

Extra

How we use Context.dev

Context.dev is the first call for every company. It decides how the whole sign-up looks, and it keeps watching afterwards.

Find the company

Typed words become the right company

Brand search turns “nitor” or “hamina wireless” into the company and its domain. The brand record then gives name, logo, colours, field, size, city and careers link: that is why every sign-up page arrives in the company’s own look.

Read its careers page

What the company tells candidates itself

The scraper reads the careers page, script-rendered ones included. Statements and needs quote it word for word, with the sentence they rest on. Two or three requests per company, about a dozen credits, every response checked and cached.

Watch it

116 monitors on 74 companies, every six hours

42 careers pages are watched for a role added or removed, and 74 web searches for funding, growth or a new site. A change shows as “changed lately” on the events screen; a person decides what to do with it.

Read a person, with consent

Their own LinkedIn address, nothing stored

An invitee pastes their own profile address; the people lookup returns roles and studies, which become lines they keep or strike. A match under 90 of 100 is refused. Nothing is cached, logged or saved on our side.

Public APIs and technologies we use

Public data and open APIs

  • PRH / YTJ open data the Finnish company register (avoindata.prh.fi)
  • Company websites and careers pages quoted word for word, with the source link
  • Public job feeds Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Recruitee, Workable, Teamtailor, Personio
  • ESCO skills API the EU skills vocabulary, for plain-language skill names
  • Local company lists Espoo Innovation Garden, Kiuas, Aalto Startup Center

Research and AI services

  • Context.dev brand colours and logo, careers-page reading, company data, page monitors
  • Exa search for news, funding and company sites
  • Anthropic Claude Haiku and Opus turn pages into quoted facts; anything not in the page word for word is dropped, and so are personal names

Build and hosting

  • Kutsu, organiser app Svelte 5, TypeScript, Vite, Python server
  • Kutsu, invitee pages plain JavaScript, no build step
  • Cloudflare Workers and KV hosting and sign-up storage
  • Playwright automated walk-through of the demo
  • Synthetic data generated people and applications; no real person anywhere
